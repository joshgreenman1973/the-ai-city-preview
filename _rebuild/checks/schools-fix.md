## Review items

The review raises two items about this essay, both in its "Problems by essay" table. It lists nothing for schools under "Fix these first" or "Not yet verified." Both sources were fetched again with curl on Oct. 20 and read as text.

1. "Seven detectors 'correctly classified 88 essays'" (review: the study found near-perfect, not perfect, accuracy; say "almost all").
   - Verdict: review right. The abstract says "the detectors accurately classified the US student essays," but the body says the accuracy "remained near perfect," and the Figure 1 caption says "near-perfect accuracy for US eighth-grade essays." "Correctly classified 88 essays" reads as all 88. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10382961/
   - Change: "The detectors were nearly perfect on the eighth-graders and labeled more than half of the non-native writers' essays as machine-made." New dossier claim C32.

2. "Paige Wyatt, 'former high school English teacher' in Chalkbeat's NYC story" (review: title unconfirmed; she is in Chalkbeat's national story).
   - Verdict: review partly wrong. The title holds: "the school where she taught high school English" and "Wyatt quit her teaching job last year." The essay never said she was in the New York City story, and the first check had already sourced her to the national story (C22, C28). The review is right that a reader could take her for a New York City teacher, since she sat in a paragraph of local reporting. https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/09/02/state-ai-guidance-schools-skirts-cheating-teachers/
   - Change: "Paige Wyatt, who taught high school English until she quit last year, told Chalkbeat for a national story on AI cheating..." The story does not say where she taught, so the essay doesn't either. New dossier claim C33.

No other facts were added. The one new quotation, the Liang authors' "strongly caution against the use of GPT detectors in evaluative or educational settings," is in C32.

- Headline kept: "The AI Pause Leaves High School Homework Untouched." New dek (25 words): "Teachers are already moving essays back into class, and the Education Department should support them and stop schools from punishing students on a detector's say-so."

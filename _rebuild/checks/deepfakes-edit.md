## Judgment calls

- Enforcement framing (expedited calendar, "more likely than not," 14-126(3) fines). Fixed and the argument adjusted. The enforcement section now lays out both routes plainly: the candidate's suit gets an automatic calendar preference and expedited proceedings, preliminary relief before an election turns on "more likely than not to succeed on the merits," and the state board's chief enforcement counsel can seek penalties of up to $1,000 or the cost of the communication, with a sentence saying it is untested whether that penalty reaches the AI label (C30, C31 and its note). Added the 14-126 link. The old line "a rule that leaves behavior unchanged, enforced by a lawsuit the target must bring ... is unlikely to stop a fake" is gone. In its place the essay concedes that "New York's enforcement is faster than ordinary litigation" and rests the case on two narrower points the statute supports: every route starts after the media is out, and an injunction only stops distribution from then on (illustrated by the Cuomo video's 2 million views on one copy despite a five-minute takedown); and the candidate's suit is open only to "a candidate whose voice or likeness appears," so it does not reach a false voting message that depicts no candidate. I think the argument holds in that narrower form. The Ridout finding is now framed as "Labeling rules have not clearly changed behavior where they exist," with his caveats kept, instead of "labeling hasn't worked."
- "The first real test is underway": softened to "The governor's race is giving the law one of its first tests," matching Wice. NY-21 left out (labeled, no complaint, adds nothing to the argument).
- "which it decides every day": cut. Replaced with what the Charter supports: "Most of those corrections would concern facts the board already has to send, such as dates, hours and poll sites." Added an honest cost: the proposal asks the board to act within hours and get it right, and the Council would first need to learn how many voters have signed up (enrollment is under "Could not confirm" in the dossier). The thesis paragraph also said the board "already runs" the text system; operation is unconfirmed, so it now says the board "is already required to run" it. The checker's 2021 ranked-choice example is not in the dossier or the confirmed list, so it stays out.
- Attorney General voter guide (Oct. 14, 2026). Left out. The check report names it only under judgment calls, with a single regional-outlet URL and no verbatim quote; it is not in the dossier or the confirmed list, which is the edit brief's bar for any fact. If Josh confirms it, it belongs in the proposal section, roughly: "Attorney General Letitia James's updated voter guide already tells voters to check AI-generated voting claims with their board of elections; the Council's change would have the board push the correction instead of waiting to be asked." It makes the proposal less novel but does not undercut it.
- Liar's dividend. Fixed. The paragraph now says the researchers "showed people hypothetical politician responses to real scandals" (C26 quote) and replaces "The dividend exists, but so far it pays out mostly against the printed word" with "These are survey findings, and in them the excuse worked better against a news story than against video."

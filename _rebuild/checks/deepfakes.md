## Errors found and fixed
- "The enforcement mechanism is a lawsuit by the candidate depicted, in state Supreme Court, for an injunction and legal fees. The plaintiff must prove the case by clear and convincing evidence." → "The statute's own remedy is a lawsuit by the candidate depicted ... for an injunction and legal fees; the case gets an expedited calendar, but the plaintiff must prove the use of deceptive media by clear and convincing evidence. A separate section lets the state Board of Elections' enforcement counsel seek civil fines for knowingly failing to label a political communication as the law requires." Why: 14-106(5)(iv)-(v) gives "an automatic calendar preference," expedited proceedings and a "more likely than not" standard for preliminary relief; Election Law 14-126(3) gives the board's chief enforcement counsel civil penalties ("up to one thousand dollars or up to the cost of the communication") for knowingly failing to identify "any political communication as required in section 14-106." The draft left out the very route the Jacobs complaint is using. https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._election_law_section_14-106 ; https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._election_law_section_14-126
- "enforced by a lawsuit the target must bring and win by clear and convincing evidence" → "enforced by a lawsuit the target must bring and win by clear and convincing evidence or by a state board that has yet to rule on its first high-profile complaint". Why: same as above.
- NYCLU "field day" warning presented as a warning against the enacted measure → "warning, aimed at her broader original language". Why: Bloomberg Law shows Harrison was criticizing the original Part MM (voters could sue; "digitization" language "so broad that it could arguably cover ordinary uses of technology"); the enacted law was narrower. https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-election-deepfake-measure-at-risk-in-ny-budget-negotiations
- "It [did not pass](thepoliticalgroup.com) this year." → "Both pieces were [cut from the budget](nysenate S9005-C) enacted in May, and the proposal did not pass this year." Why: primary source now confirms. The enacted PPGG budget bill, S.9005-C/A.10005-C (chapter 55 of 2026, signed May 27), lists Part R ("preventing voter suppression and deception in elections") and Part S ("non-consensual materially deceptive media prior to an election") as "Intentionally Omitted." Saved at `checks/pdfs/deepfakes/S9005C-ppgg-budget-2026.html`. https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9005/amendment/C
- "No ruling had been reported as of late September." → "as of mid-October." Why: Gizmodo (Oct. 16, 2026) still describes the complaint without an outcome; searches on Oct. 19 found no ruling. https://gizmodo.com/137-million-has-been-spent-on-ai-generated-ads-ahead-of-midterms-only-27-included-a-disclaimer-2000827138
- Charter 1057-d "but only for a fixed list of purposes ... absentee voting, mail-ballot deadlines" → "for a fixed list of purposes ... in-person absentee voting, mail-ballot application deadlines". Why: the section requires notices for four listed purposes; it does not say "only," and it does not bar other messages. Items (2) and (3) are in-person absentee voting and the deadline for the mail-voting *application*. Text from the nyc-charter tool (American Legal), listed at https://www.nyccfb.info/law/charter/
- Link fixes: the two APSA-report links pointed to a Stanford press release that says nothing about Adams, deepfakes or "AI slop." They now point to the report PDF (https://apsanet.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Lee-TF-APSA-AI-Report-2026-Tucker-Persily.pdf); quotes confirmed on pp. 41 (Adams/ElevenLabs, "Partially in response"), 73 ("played little role," "readily recognized as fake"), 78 ("temporal validity," "likely to degrade," "still do not instinctively disbelieve"). The $137 million link now points to Wesleyan's own Oct. 15 release instead of Gizmodo.

## Judgment calls for the editor
- Enforcement framing. The essay's argument ("a lawsuit the target must bring") understated the legal tools: the candidate's suit is expedited and preliminary relief needs only "more likely than not." Whether the 14-126(3) fine reaches the AI label, as opposed to the "paid for by" line, is untested. I put both in at their smallest. The essay's case that labels can't move fast enough still stands, but it is weaker than the draft made it look.
- "The first real test is underway in the governor's race": defensible (Wice's "one of the first times"). There is also an NY-21 AI video from August 2026; it was reportedly labeled and drew no complaint.
- "The board's only judgment would be whether a stated fact about voting is wrong, which it decides every day" is opinion presented as fact. The city Board of Elections' record on accuracy (for example, the 2021 ranked-choice count error) cuts against "decides every day" being easy. Also, the board is a state-created body; whether the Council can impose this duty by local law is plausible (1057-d itself is a 2016 local law) but not settled in the essay.
- Attorney General Letitia James's updated voter guide (reported Oct. 14, 2026) already warns voters to check AI-generated voting claims with their board of elections. That is relevant to "New York City has that channel on paper" and to fairness about what officials already do. https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/10/14/new-york-warns-voters-to-verify-ai-generated-election-claims-before-sharing/
- "The dividend exists, but so far it pays out mostly against the printed word" rests on survey experiments with hypothetical responses, not field evidence; consider "In experiments, the dividend..."
- The Cuomo-video labeling conflict (Common Cause/NYT vs. 404 Media) remains unresolved; the essay reports both, which is fair.
- Hochul's "in the run-up to elections" timing comes from the governor's Jan. 13 press release, not the linked Gothamist piece.

## Errors found and fixed
- Dek: "The data that reaches immigration agents increasingly moves through vendors and databases" → "Much of the data that reaches immigration agents moves through vendors and databases." Why: no source in the dossier or the essay measures a trend over time. The essay shows the routes exist, not that they are growing.
- "A growing share of the information that reaches Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) never passes through an employee's hands" → "Much of the information..." Why: same problem. Nothing measures a "share," let alone a growing one.
- "a state crime analysis center had searched it for a 'DHS Agent'" → "had searched it with the stated reason 'SWB 25-03-01 DHS Agent'". Why: the report quotes the full reason string and only reads it as "apparently referring to" a DHS employee. The old wording turned the report's guess into fact. https://www.stopspying.org/s/2026-09-30_Flock-Favors_STOP-LatinoJustice_Report.pdf
- The S.T.O.P./LatinoJustice finding now names the evidence's limits: "found in search logs it called 'an incomplete, redacted snapshot' that as recently as July...". Why: the brief asks that the report be presented as advocacy work with its limits. The essay already said "both advocacy groups" but left out that the logs are partial and redacted, which the report says itself. The report also gives no counts.
- "Albany has a pending bill that would require..." → "A bill in Albany, stuck in committee since April, would require..." Why: S9890 was referred to the Senate Consumer Protection Committee on April 13, 2026, and nothing has happened since. The 2026 session is over. "Pending" is technically true but suggests it is moving. https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9890

## Judgment calls for the editor
- "But New York has already shown that writing rules into systems works." The UW study supports this only for one channel, the DMV-through-Nlets route. The same study says ICE and CBP traffic stops are common across New York and that the broker route could not be tested. Consider "can work."
- Final paragraph: "with results reported in the audits Executive Order 13 already requires." EO 13 required a one-time report by May 7, plus ongoing internal review with no further reporting. It does not require any audit of vendors or search logs. The sentence reads as a proposal, but "already requires" could mislead. I could not confirm whether the May 7 reports were published.
- Flock's 7-day default and offense filtering are the vendor's own announcements. S.T.O.P. called them a "crisis PR stunt." The essay attributes them to Flock, which is fair. The chart label "Flock default since Aug. 2026" is Flock's claim, and the post says required case codes "will" come.
- The essay says a camera vendor falls outside the identifying information law "unless the chief privacy officer designates it." Whether any such designation exists could not be confirmed either way. Separately, EO 13's own definition of "contractor" is broader ("a person who has been awarded a contract by a city agency"), but it applies only within the order.
- The identifying information law was quoted from the 2017 enacted text. It was amended in 2022, and I did not compare it against the current codified Admin. Code text.
- Fairness: the "best case against" section is fair. It concedes that fingerprints, brokers and federal benefit data are beyond a city's reach.

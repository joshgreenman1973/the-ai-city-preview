## Errors found and fixed
- "Another 3,839 got no referral at all that year. The reason the City recorded for every one of those 3,839: 'Data not in CAPS.'" → "Another 3,839 had no referral recorded in the system that year. The report gives no reason for any of them: its only entry under 'Reason for No Referral' is 'Data not in CAPS,' meaning the system holds no reason, though Local Law 3 asks for one." Why: "Data not in CAPS" is a placeholder, not a reason. The same single row covers every non-referred applicant in all five reports (4,372; 4,031; 4,249; 3,779; 3,839). The fiscal 2022 report says plainly: "HRA has not operationalized making client-level designations for why referrals are not made in CAPS." The fiscal 2026 report also says many providers take "direct referrals that are not captured in CAPS," and it counts only referrals made within the year. So "no referral at all" overstated what the report shows. https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hra/downloads/pdf/news/HRA-Local-Law-3-CFY2025-08272026.pdf ; https://www.nyc.gov/assets/hra/downloads/pdf/news/HRA-Local-Law-3-CFY2022-08302022.pdf
- Skyline stat "got no referral in fiscal 2026" → "had no referral recorded in fiscal 2026." Chart row 'Got no referral (reason recorded: "Data not in CAPS")' → "Got no referral in CAPS (no reason recorded)." Chart title "last year" → "within the year." Why: same as above.

## Unverifiable, cut or softened
- Whether the SVA thresholds have changed since 2019 is unverifiable: the "SVA Criteria Fact Sheet" is posted only inside the CAPS portal. I softened this with the "last posted in 2019" fix above.
- "The data feeds have a known blind spot" (applicants without a Social Security or Medicaid number) is kept. The FAQ says such clients get "NO matches" and "no access to any data matches for homelessness." Strictly, that passage describes the CAPS search function, and the essay infers that the SVA's data feeds are affected. The inference is reasonable but not stated by the City. The essay correctly leaves the effect on grades as an open question.

## Better statistic available
- For context on the 3,839, the fiscal 2022 report broke down why applicants went unreferred: 43% had been approved within the last four months, and many were in mental-health-only categories with no CAPS referral entity at the time. Later reports drop that breakdown. If the editor wants to blunt the "no referral" figure fairly, the fiscal 2022 breakdown is the only published one. Its shares should not be applied to 2026.

## Judgment calls for the editor
- "New York has done the harder half of the job by writing its rule down." The priority order is public, but the SVA's actual criteria (the "SVA Criteria Fact Sheet") are posted only inside the CAPS portal, and the public manual dates from 2019. "Writing its rule down" is defensible, but the rule is only partly public. Consider whether the essay should say so.
- The "two-line fix" now says the law already asks for a no-referral reason. That arguably strengthens the essay: HRA is not meeting an existing requirement, rather than the Council needing new language. The editor may want to reframe the second item as enforcement rather than amendment. I made only the minimal factual fix.
- The 3,839 figure is still the skyline stat. Read correctly, it means "had no referral recorded in CAPS by June 30 among people approved during the year." Many were approved in the last few months of the year, and some were referred outside CAPS. It is a fair number but a soft one for a headline stat.
- The HUD litigation is live. The stay pends appeal, and NAEH's emergency motion to block awards had no ruling as of Oct. 19. Recheck before publication.
- The Allegheny study is a conference paper (AIES 2024) and arXiv preprint, not a journal article. The essay's "a study" is fine.

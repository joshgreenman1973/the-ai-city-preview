## Word count

## Judgment calls
- **Int. 3 overlap.** Reframed the proposal as amending the bill's focus. The essay now says plainly that Int. 3 already asks how data centers would affect rates, but only data centers built in the city, which the Gold Book forecasts none of. The amendment keeps the study and changes its subject: strike the site search; estimate how much of the statewide cost of serving large loads elsewhere in the state could land on Con Edison bills; file that estimate with the Public Service Commission; and add a yearly report on megawatts of storage and transmission in service in Zone J against Con Edison's projected shortfall. The filing and the yearly report are the parts Int. 3 does not do. The section subhead changed from "A smaller, better bill" to "What the Council could change."
- **Case 26-E-0045 and Energize NY.** Dropped Energize NY. The proposal names only Case 26-E-0045, described by its title in EO 62 ("interconnection reforms for large loads") and linked to the order. In its place the paragraph quotes EO 62's stated policy that upgrade costs for large loads "should not be paid for by every-day New Yorkers" (C16), which is verified and does the same work.
- **Berkeley Lab/Brattle residential finding.** Added "overall" ("overall retail prices fall after inflation") and a new sentence: the relationship "was smaller and lost statistical significance for residential prices alone," linked to the American Public Power Association summary already in the dossier (C22 secondary source). The supply-constraint warning stays.
- **Legistar committee quirk.** Left as "in committee," which is safe.
- **Micron 1,000 MW (summer).** Left as written.
- **"About a second of microwave time."** Moot; the chatbot-energy paragraph was cut (see below).
- **Siena sample.** No sample size stated; left alone.
- **Tom's Hardware headline.** Not used; nothing to change.

- "Con Edison is under a Public Service Commission order to report every six months" became "Con Edison updates its estimate of the city's reliability needs every six months." The confirmed fact is the six-month update; the essay no longer names which order requires it.

- "As of this week" became "As of Oct. 19."
- "percent" changed to "%" throughout, per the edit brief and stylebook.
- The ending is now the yearly shortfall report, with Con Edison's 2033 and 2036 figures, instead of a two-part summary sentence.
- Chart title sharpened to "Almost all the large new load forecast for 2040 is upstate, and none is in New York City." Data unchanged. Claim map updated.

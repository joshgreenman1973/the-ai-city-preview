## Judgment calls

I moved the instruction first ("Start with 311 ...") so the parenthetical qualifies the proposal instead of interrupting it, and tightened "the auditors' test was of phone calls" to "the auditors tested phone calls." Source: C32.
2. **DCWP's rebuttal on resources and the workbook.** Fixed. The draft said "The staff and the workbook already exist," which runs against DCWP's stated objection. I cut that sentence. The case-for-complaints section now has a paragraph stating that, in its formal response, DCWP said its two attorneys carry heavy workloads elsewhere, that it got no additional resources for Local Law 144, and that the OTI workbooks "are helpful on complex cases, but are not necessary to use in all circumstances." I confirmed that wording and the resource language against the downloaded audit PDF (checks/pdfs/landlord-algorithm/osc-2024-N-6, DCWP response, report pp. 26-29). The proposal now answers the objection instead of brushing past it: it concedes the workbook may not be needed in every case, argues that a fixed checklist is what makes one year comparable to the next, and says the City should pay for the sweep in DCWP's budget rather than leave it to two attorneys with other caseloads. "Paid for in its budget" is also in the thesis paragraph so the reader knows from the start that the proposal costs money.
3. **"They read company websites and placed phone calls."** Fixed by cutting the merge. The rebuttal paragraph now says only that the auditors, "using only public information," found at least 17 potential violations, and that the City "can look on the employer's website, as the auditors did." The phone calls stay in the opening and the 311 proposal, where they belong.
4. **Signing date.** Left Oct. 16, 2025, per the court record and the attorney general's sworn answer.
5. **Headline argument.** Changed for style (above), but the argument is the same and is labeled as such.
6. **Fair Chance for Housing Act.** Kept as one sentence of context. I considered cutting it as material the argument doesn't need, but it is the essay's only New York City tenant-screening rule and carries a source link. Josh may want to cut it (see below).

# fiscal fact-check
Checked: Oct. 19, 2026. Statements checked: 62.

Source copies (PDFs, fetched HTML and extracted text) are in `_rebuild/checks/pdfs/fiscal/`.

## Errors found and fixed
- "including an expected lease by Anthropic, the company whose model wrote this issue, at 330 Hudson Street" → "and AI companies are among the new tenants: in July, Anthropic, the company whose model wrote this issue, [finalized a lease](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/07/08/anthropic-finalizes-hudson-square-lease-as-it-grows-in-nyc/) for all of 330 Hudson Street." Why: the draft repeated the Comptroller's May 21 wording ("the expected Anthropic lease of 330 Hudson Street," report p. 11), but that is out of date. The Real Deal reported on July 8, 2026, citing the New York Times, that Anthropic was "sealing the deal for a full building in Hudson Square" and "is leasing the 16-story building at 330 Hudson Street from AEW Capital Management." CoStar and CRE Daily report the same deal (about 466,000 square feet). The draft also folded a 2026 deal into a sentence about 2025 leasing, and the fix separates them. Added to the dossier as C27.
- "Private employment in the city ends up 259,000 jobs below the baseline in early 2029" → "falls as far as 259,000 jobs below the baseline in early 2029". Why: 259,000 is the deepest point, not where the scenario ends. The report says the gap "reaches 259,000 in the first quarter of 2029, with only a small recovery by the end of 2030" (p. 26), https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/AI-and-NYCs-Fiscal-Future.pdf
- "Mamdani has described 'a $12 billion deficit,'" → "Mamdani says the City was facing 'a $12 billion deficit' when he took office,". Why: in the June 30 transcript he describes the deficit he inherited ("It was January ... Our city was reckoning with a $12 billion deficit"). He is not describing a current gap, and the draft read as if he were. https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/06/transcript-_mayor-mamdani-announces-handshake-agreement-on-fisca.html
- "reserves came to about 8.5 percent of tax revenue" → "reserves were expected to equal about 8.5 percent of tax revenue at the end of fiscal 2026". Why: the report gives this as a projection: "are expected to hold a combined balance of $7.2 billion, or 8.5 percent of budgeted tax revenues" (p. 8). The final fiscal 2026 balance for the retiree health trust is not out yet; the Comptroller's August comments say a new statement "will be available later on this year."
- "16 percent of the city's wages but more than two-thirds of its dividends ... and nearly 83 percent of its capital gains" → "16 percent of resident filers' wages ... of their dividends ... of their capital gains". Why: the shares in the Fiscal Note are of income reported by full-year resident filers, not of all wages earned in the city. That matters in a paragraph about commuters. https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/The-NYC-Personal-Income-Tax-Before-and-After-the-Pandemic.pdf

## Judgment calls for the editor
- The essay's frame, that the "main thing AI has done to the City budget is fill it," treats the whole $7.8 billion in securities-industry taxes as AI money. DiNapoli credits AI spending along with M&A and trading volume. Nobody attributes a specific share of the windfall to AI. The sentence is an argument, not a measurement, and might be softened to "helped fill it."
- The skyline stat says "$14 billion in lost City tax revenue." The figure is relative to a baseline and excludes the property tax (roughly half of tax revenue). "Lost" may read as a drop from today's collections. Consider "$14 billion below baseline."
- "The two good scenarios together carry 50 percent" counts the baseline (AI-Empowered Economy) as "good." The report describes it as modest growth "with little disruptive effect," so it is neutral rather than good.
- Fairness on Proposal 5: the essay presents it as movement in the right direction. Critics including Andrew Rein of the Citizens Budget Commission plan to vote no because 12 percent is too low, and the Comptroller calls the withdrawal rules "weak." The essay's "the details are still open" is fair but mild.
- Disclosure: the essay correctly notes that Anthropic made the model writing the issue. The OSC report also mentions Anthropic's expected IPO. The essay doesn't use that, which is fine.

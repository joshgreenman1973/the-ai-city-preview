## Errors found and fixed
- Section head "A fix already on the governor's desk" → "A fix already through the Legislature". Why: the bill has passed both houses but has not been delivered to the governor; the essay's own body says so. Senate bill page action history ends "Jun 04, 2026 | returned to assembly; passed senate," status "Passed Senate & Assembly," no delivered/signed/vetoed entry, https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A9581
- "a bill awaiting the governor could do it with one addition" → "a bill the Legislature passed in June could do it with one addition". Why: same; in New York, a bill isn't before the governor until delivered, same URL.
- "Hiring fell for everyone after a busy 2022: ... but hires of 35- to 44-year-olds fell 21 percent too" → "Hiring fell for older workers too after a busy 2022: ... and hires of 35- to 44-year-olds fell 21 percent." Why: my QWI pull shows that across all private industries in the five boroughs, hires of 35- to 44-year-olds rose 4.9 percent from 2022 to 2025 (543,679 to 570,079), so "everyone" was too broad. The professional-services figures (22-24 down 29.7 percent, 35-44 down 20.7 percent, all ages down 23.1 percent) are correct. Census QWI API, query below.
- "In 2025 it had 8,552" → "8,553". Why: the four-quarter average is 8,552.5; the essay rounds every other figure conventionally (27,313.5 → 27,314). Census QWI API.
- "employers have no definition of 'automation' to work from" → "no clear definition". Why: Hunton says employers "may lack clarity on how 'technological innovation or automation' is defined", not that there is no definition, https://natlawreview.com/article/new-york-warn-act-no-ai-related-layoffs-reported-first-year-adding-ai-related
- "the best evidence so far says AI's first effect on New York's work force is a slowdown in hiring" → "suggests". Why: the Stanford authors call their findings "descriptive patterns, not causal estimates," and the New York figures are the essay's own analysis with no attribution to AI. "Says" was stronger than the evidence, https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/
- Dek: "the early damage shows up in hiring" → "the early signs show up in hiring". Why: same reason; the essay later says the evidence stops short of "proving AI is the cause."

## Judgment calls for the editor
- "Lowest of the 25 largest metros" is a ranking of point estimates. New York (17.3) sits about one point below Riverside (18.4), and single-round metro standard errors run roughly 0.6 to 2 points, so the gap may not be statistically meaningful. "Among the lowest" would be safer; I left it because the arithmetic is right. Also, the Census file is labeled "Top 25 MSA"; I could not load the downloads page text (it renders with JavaScript) to confirm it is defined as the 25 most populous metros.
- The QCEW link points only to the Manhattan county file, but the sentence describes five-borough sums. The link backs part of the claim; the methodology note should list all five county files.
- "Advertising" in the QCEW sentence is NAICS 5418, "advertising, public relations and related services." It's close enough, but the methodology should say so.
- The junior-analyst illustration (12 hires instead of 20 leaves "no previously filled position to report") is the writer's reading of the bill text. An employer or the Labor Department could read "positions previously filled" to cover seats filled in the prior year. The sentence hedges with "may," but it is an argument, not a fact.
- "Hochul usually acts on major bills in the last weeks of the year" is accurate for the timing of decisions. Note that the timing really depends on when the Legislature delivers the bill. Delivery itself hasn't happened.
- The fairness balance is reasonable: Yale, the Stanford caveats, the pre-ChatGPT decline, the finance counterexample and the BTOS low adoption are all fairly stated. The softened dek and paragraph 3 now match the essay's own conclusion that causation isn't proved.

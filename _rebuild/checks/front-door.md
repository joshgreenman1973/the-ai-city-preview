# front-door fact-check
Checked: Oct. 19, 2026. Statements checked: 74.

Sources were downloaded and read directly (saved under `_rebuild/checks/pdfs/front-door/`); the Local Law 35 dataset was queried directly. Web searches were used for currency checks on Denver's Sunny and any MyCity successor.

## Errors found and fixed
- "The contract is paid through the end of October, and as of this writing nobody has said whether Sunny will survive." → "The contract is paid through the end of October. A city technology official told the council the service would not continue without the contract; Johnston's office said it would meet with the council to find a way to keep it running. As of this writing, no deal has been announced." Why: GovTech/Denver Post reports Jenny Schiavone of Denver Technology Services said the city wouldn't be "continuing the service," and the mayor's spokesman said the office would seek a solution; searches through Oct. 19 found no deal. "Nobody has said" was wrong — both sides had said something. https://www.govtech.com/artificial-intelligence/denver-ai-chatbot-sunny-may-go-dark-after-contract-vote
- "with most misses being answers that covered part of a question" → "with many misses being answers that covered only part of a question." Why: GDS says "it's often that GOV.UK Chat is able to answer some but not all aspects" — "often," not "most." https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2026/03/16/5-things-we-learned-testing-gov-uk-chat-an-ai-assistant-for-government/
- "The bot refuses anything outside published guidance" → "The bot draws only on published guidance." Why: GDS says Chat "exclusively draws on guidance published on GOV.UK"; it does not say it refuses everything else (same URL).
- "the bot cannot yet hand a user to a human adviser" → "as of March the bot could not hand a user to a human adviser." Why: the limitation is stated in the March 2026 post ("isn't something we currently support"); the May launch post does not update it, so the present tense was not verifiable (same URL).
- "the British team found that people trust a government bot more than they should" → "...that some people trust..." Why: GDS 2024: "some users underestimated or dismissed the inaccuracy risks ... because of the credibility ... of the GOV.UK brand." https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2024/01/18/the-findings-of-our-first-generative-ai-experiment-gov-uk-chat/
- "a 92 percent customer satisfaction index and interpreters in 175 languages" → "311 scores 92 percent on its customer satisfaction index and offers phone service in 175 languages." Why: the MMR says "available via phone in 175 languages" (not "interpreters"), and the 92 percent index is "Customer satisfaction index (311 only)," not specific to the phone line. https://www.nyc.gov/assets/operations/downloads/pdf/mmr2026/311.pdf
- "warns that agencies already lean on unsupervised machine translation" → "warns that some agencies already lean on..." Why: report says "Some agencies noted reliance on unsupervised machine translation tools." https://www.nyc.gov/assets/immigrants/downloads/pdf/FY25-Language-Access-Annual-Report.pdf
- "Typing or saying 'agent' should reach a person at once, as Denver's voice bot proposal promised." → "Asking for a human should reach a person at once, as Denver's voice bot proposal promised." Why: 9News: transfer happens if a caller says "human," "live agent" or "a person"; nothing about typing or the word "agent" alone. https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/people-call-311-denver-help-035324965.html
- "by mid-2024 it handled 12 percent of 311 inquiries" → "accounted for 12 percent." Why: Westword: "Sunny engagements account for 12 percent of all 311 inquiries, according to the city" — engagements, not resolved inquiries. https://www.westword.com/news/denver-ai-chatbot-sunny-knows-basics-cant-help-on-hot-topics-20891202
- DYCD chatbot "returns staff-written answers from a fixed knowledge base" → "returns predefined answers from a staff-maintained knowledge base." Why: filing says "predefined answers" and "Staff worked to capture and maintain information ... in a knowledge base"; it was upgraded in 2025 to Azure Custom Question Answering, so "fixed" overstated it (dossier C28). https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/jaw4-yuem.json?$limit=1000

## Judgment calls for the editor
- Markup lede: the article also says the bot at one point told a reporter landlords did have to accept vouchers before 10 staffers were all told no. The essay's version is accurate but omits that the bot was inconsistent, which arguably strengthens the essay's point.
- "the City never published how often the bot was right" is an absence claim. No published accuracy figure turned up in the Markup, City & State, the LL35 filing or the MMR, and Hoodline reported in March 2026 that the City was still withholding records on the chatbot. Reasonable to keep, but it is a negative.
- "MyCity failed on rules about rights and money, with no limits and no published testing": "no limits" is true of the bot as The Markup tested it in March 2024; Fraser said a scope restriction was added about two weeks later. Consider "at first, with no limits."
- "Denver shows the budget logic: once a bot exists, staffed hours start to look optional" is the writer's inference from the October 2025 cuts. Denver framed the cut as a budget move and pointed callers to Sunny; it did not say the bot justified the cut.
- DYCD's chatbot is offered as a contrast to MyCity's design, but the City's filing classes DYCD's bot as "Fully autonomous" too, same as MyCity. The contrast holds on the answers (predefined vs. generated), not on autonomy.
- The GOV.UK "90 percent" is self-graded; the essay says so. The 76 percent is GDS's "earliest benchmark," not necessarily the first pilot's result; the essay's "rose from 76 percent to 90 percent" matches the source wording.
- The Denver Post/GovTech says "Tuesday," and Oct. 13, 2026 is a Tuesday (Monday was a holiday), so the date holds. A search-tool summary suggested the vote might be from 2025; that is wrong. The article is dated Oct. 14, 2026, and only a file photo is from 2025.

# face-and-plate fact-check
Checked: Oct. 19, 2026. Statements checked: 78.

Source copies saved under `_rebuild/checks/pdfs/face-and-plate/` (OIG-NYPD 2022, 2024 and April 2026 reports; DOI Feb. 2025 testimony; Legal Aid letter; LPR, DAS, drone and facial recognition policies; Local Law 55 text; NISTIR 8280 and 8429; NYPD POST Act and facial recognition pages; Comptroller ShotSpotter audit page; NIST FRTE demographics page; Fight for the Future map).

## Errors found and fixed
- "42 surveillance policies" (skyline stat and value), "Forty-two policies, no tallies" (subhead), "now lists 42 of them" → 41 in each place. Why: the NYPD POST Act page as served Oct. 19, 2026 displays 41 policies. The Electromagnetic Weapons Detection System row (which linked to the wrong PDF) and an old 2023 Situational Awareness Cameras row both sit inside HTML comments and do not render. I confirmed this by parsing the downloaded HTML and by counting the rendered rows in a browser: 41, and "Electromagnetic" does not appear on the rendered page. https://www.nyc.gov/site/nypd/about/about-nypd/policy/post-act.page
- "said the policies were so 'general and generic' that it could not conduct the audit" → "said most of the policies were so 'general and generic' that it could not conduct the audit". Why: the report says "the vast majority of the IUPs produced by NYPD were general and generic in part." https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doi/reports/pdf/2022/20PostActRelease_Rpt_11032022.pdf
- "Chasing the unconfirmed and unfounded alerts took 426.9 officer hours that month" → "By the auditors' estimate, chasing the unconfirmed and unfounded alerts took 426.9 hours of police time that month." Why: the auditors estimated the figure from deployment and closing times because "NYPD does not directly collect or analyze this information." It measures response time, not officer-hours; the audit says the shift count doubles if two officers responded. https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/audit-report-on-the-new-york-city-police-departments-oversight-of-its-agreement-with-shotspotter-inc-for-the-gunshot-detection-and-location-system/
- "The most accurate tended to have smaller gaps" → "NIST expected the most accurate to have smaller gaps". Why: NISTIR 8280 says more accurate algorithms "will be expected therefore to have smaller demographic differentials." That is a stated expectation, not a measured finding. https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2019/NIST.IR.8280.pdf
- "including one from Idemia ... showed none that NIST could detect" → "showed no false-positive gap that NIST could detect". Why: NIST found Idemia's identification algorithms had undetectable false-positive differentials. It did not say there were no differentials of any kind. Same URL.
- "searches run only against arrest and parole photos" → "searches run against arrest and parole photos unless top commanders approve an exception". Why: the Feb. 4, 2026 policy allows comparisons "against images outside the photo repository" with approval from the Chief of Department, Chief of Detectives and the Deputy Commissioner of Intelligence and Counterterrorism. This matters because the Williams and Columbia cases both turn on searches outside that repository. https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nypd/downloads/pdf/public_information/post-final/Facial-Recognition-NYPD-Impact-and-Use-Policy-2.4.26-FINAL.pdf
- "The current term [runs through December 2027]" → "The renewal the department signed [runs through December 2027]". Why: the linked Comptroller release (Feb. 5, 2025) is the office declining to register that renewal on procedural grounds. It says the contract "contemplated a three-year renewal (from December 2024 through December 2027)." I found no source on whether a corrected version was later registered, and Checkbook NYC blocked automated access. So the end date is confirmed as the signed term, but I could not confirm that this term is in force. https://comptroller.nyc.gov/newsroom/comptroller-lander-declines-to-register-shotspotter-contract-renewal
- THE CITY link updated to https://www.thecityreporter.nyc/2025/07/18/nypd-fdny-clearview-ai-ban-columbia-palestinian-protest/ because the old domain now 301-redirects there (the outlet renamed itself). The content supports the sentence.

## Judgment calls for the editor
- The 41 count reflects the page today. The writer counted 42 earlier the same day, with the Electromagnetic Weapons Detection row treated as displayed. Either the NYPD hid that row today or the writer misread the HTML. Whichever it was, the count can move again; consider "about 40" or rechecking at publication.
- "The NYPD rejected 14" is the original response. The essay then gives the April 2026 status, so the reader isn't misled. But the subhead "no tallies" and the line "It never made the department count" sit beside Local Law 55, which does require counts. The essay later says so, so this is an argument, not an error.
- ShotSpotter "met that 90 percent target in most boroughs": accurate (all but Manhattan), but the vendor and the NYPD describe the 90% standard as an "accuracy rate." The essay's "whether the system missed gunfire" framing follows the audit's description, which is fair.
- Fairness: the NYPD's facial recognition page also says NIST "has identified major improvements in the accuracy of facial recognition software" over the last half decade. NIST's 2022 summary partly supports this for false negatives but says false-positive gaps persist. The department's case section could mention it if the editor wants the strongest version of the opposing view. I did not add it.
- The Williams account is Legal Aid's. The essay attributes it properly ("according to his lawyer," "as Legal Aid describes it"). The NYPD has not publicly confirmed the SAU detail.
- "Fight for the Future ... lists about two dozen ... with bans or restrictions": the tracker's "Bans" category has 25 pins, and some are restrictions (for example Anchorage). The tracker has no update date and shows Baltimore's expired ordinance as a pin.

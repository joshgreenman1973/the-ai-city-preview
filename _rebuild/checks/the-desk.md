## Errors found and fixed
- "Staff in four offices under New York City's mayor have been using ChatGPT since March 2024." → "Staff in four offices under New York City's mayor use ChatGPT, the first of them since March 2024." Why: date_first_use 2024/03 covers the entry as a whole. The filing's updated_desc says the other three offices started in CY2025: Talent and Workforce Development and Nonprofit Services in January 2025, Community Mental Health in August 2025. https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/jaw4-yuem.json?$limit=1000
- "Gov. Kathy Hochul opened AI Pro ... to a workforce of more than 100,000." → "took AI Pro ... past a 1,200-user pilot, offering it to more than 50 state agencies and a workforce of more than 100,000." Why: the release opens training to the whole workforce. AI Pro goes to "the 50+ state agencies and entities ITS supports," and training is required "for agencies that elect to use AI Pro." Adoption is opt-in by agency, so "opened ... to a workforce" overstated it. https://governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-delivers-artificial-intelligence-training-tool-new-york-state-workforce
- "Pennsylvania's pilot participants said they saved 95 minutes on the days they used ChatGPT, with no outside measurement" → "said they saved an average of 95 minutes ... and the state's release describes no outside measurement." Why: the source says "an average of 95 minutes on days they used generative AI." Also, "no outside measurement" was stated as a fact, but we can only show that the release describes none. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/oa/newsroom/icymi--shapiro-administration-s-generative-ai-pilot-for-state-wo.html
- "CSEA reads the law the same way: it now covers employees of 'any county, city, town, village.'" → "CSEA read the December law the same way: its protections 'will now extend to employees of any county, city, town, village.'" Why: CSEA's Jan. 9, 2026, item describes S.7599 (Ch. 711) and predates the Feb. 13 chapter amendment. "Now covers" is not CSEA's wording. Its verbatim phrase is "will now extend to employees of any county, city, town, village." https://cseany.org/?p=20216
- "DC 37, which represents nearly 90,000 City workers" → "whose main City contract covers nearly 90,000 workers." Why: the 90,000 figure is the coverage of the 2021–26 contract ("This agreement will cover nearly 90,000 municipal employees"), not DC 37's total membership. https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2023/02/mayor-adams-dc-37-tentative-contract-agreement-providing-fair-wage-increases-and
- "Critics have faulted the state" → "A TechPolicy.Press commentary has faulted the state." Why: the only source is a single commentary by Javaid Iqbal Sofi (June 5, 2026). https://www.techpolicy.press/state-ai-rollouts-are-outrunning-their-own-governance
- "with the first due by Dec. 30" → "by Dec. 30, 2026." This adds the year, which the sentence lacked. My own reading of the statute is in the Confirmed section.

## Judgment calls for the editor
- Local Law 25 "became law in January without Mayor Zohran Mamdani's signature." This is literally true. But the Council passed it Dec. 18, 2025, under Adams, and it was presented then. It lapsed into law after 30 days without action, a window that ran across both mayors. Mamdani "returned [it] unsigned" Jan. 20. Readers may take this as a deliberate Mamdani choice; consider "became law in January without a mayor's signature."
- Skyline stat "43 percent ... used AI at work by late 2025" drops "at least a few times a year." The body text keeps the qualifier. Consider adding it to the skyline if space allows.
- "Whether a drafting assistant even counts is an open question": fair, and the Manhattan Institute brief supports it. The essay's argument that the law "says nothing against giving a worker a tool" is the writer's reading, reasonably framed.
- AI Pro and "state-built": the release says "developed by ITS and powered by Google Gemini," so "state-built" is fair.
- The CSEA statement was about Ch. 711. Ch. 711's 80(10)(b)(ii) did not literally name city employees; Ch. 86 did. The essay's chronology is right, and CSEA is now framed as reading the December law.
- DC 37 contract: it expires in about three weeks. If a deal lands before publication, the "next contract" framing needs a refresh.
- The 2025 Local Law 35 filing gives one frequency ("Daily") for all four offices, but its own description says Talent and Workforce Development uses ChatGPT monthly. "Lists the use as daily" accurately describes the filing.

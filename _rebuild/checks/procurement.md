## Unverifiable, cut or softened
- Why the renewal registered on Feb. 13, 2025, eight days after Lander returned it on Feb. 5. No source says what changed. SoundThinking's Feb. 6 statement called it "an easily reconcilable technicality that we are currently working to address with NYPD" and predicted registration. Checkbook shows the registered contract with the original three-year term (Dec. 13, 2024-Dec. 12, 2027), $21,853,900, now at version 3. The most likely explanation is that the option was dropped or the missing approvals were obtained, but that is inference. Nothing suggests a mayoral override: § 328's override applies only to corruption objections, and this was a return on procedural grounds. The essay states only the dates, which are correct. This settles the face-and-plate checker's open question: the renewal is registered and in force (that checker could not reach Checkbook).
- Whether the rider is mandatory, and its exact terms: not public. The essay now says "recommend ... not required," which matches the testimony's "recommends." OTI should be asked for a copy before publication.

## Judgment calls for the editor
- The rider changes the essay's argument. The draft treated the City as having no AI terms. Under oath, OTI says it has a rider in use, recommended for all software contracts. The thesis survives: the rider is unpublished and not mandatory, and its stated topics (IP, security, data use, privacy) do not include performance measurement, false-positive reporting or termination. But the "What the City can do" section now asks the City to publish, extend and require the rider rather than write one. The editor may want the dek and the section to say so more directly. Ask OTI for the rider's text and whether any agency is required to use it.
- "added during the 2021 renewal": the audit's text says the SLA came "when the agreement was renewed in 2021." Its footnote 8 says "the performance target was added to the contract in March 2022." I left the wording because it follows the audit's main text.
- "The ShotSpotter audit identified the measurement problem in 2024, and the contract was renewed anyway." This is accurate, but the NYPD and SoundThinking dispute the audit's framing. SoundThinking says its contracts "contain stringent service level agreement (SLA) criteria with ... financial penalties." The essay already notes the 94% claim. Its case-against section does not give the vendor's view of the audit.
- "In its October 2025 progress report": the document is dated Oct. 16, 2025. The state comptroller's August 2026 follow-up audit says it was published in December 2025. Either is defensible. "October 2025" matches the document.
- The progress report quietly dropped "Terms" from Action 6.4's title ("Standards, Terms, or Guidance" became "Standards or Guidance"). This might interest the writer. I did not add it.
- The "relaying the police department's assessment" wording on the Los Angeles document is cautious. In that city, the CAO is the budget office, and the sentence is ambiguous about whose conclusion it is.
- The face-and-plate essay softened its ShotSpotter end date to "the renewal the department signed." Checkbook now confirms the renewal is registered and runs to Dec. 12, 2027, so that essay can say the current term runs through December 2027.

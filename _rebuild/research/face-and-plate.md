C21. NIST's 2019 study of 189 algorithms from 99 developers found demographic differentials in the majority of face recognition algorithms; in one-to-many searches of an FBI mugshot set, false positives were higher for African American women; the most accurate algorithms can be expected to have smaller differentials, and some, including Idemia's, had undetectable false-positive differentials in identification. NIST said system owners should know their algorithm and that it will usually be informative to measure accuracy of the operational algorithm on operational images.
- Source: NIST news release, "NIST Study Evaluates Effects of Race, Age, Sex on Face Recognition Software," Dec. 19, 2019 (updated Feb. 3, 2025) — https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2019/12/nist-study-evaluates-effects-race-age-sex-face-recognition-software ; NISTIR 8280, "Face Recognition Vendor Test (FRVT) Part 3: Demographic Effects," Dec. 2019 — https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2019/NIST.IR.8280.pdf
- Quote (release): "The NIST study evaluated 189 software algorithms from 99 developers — a majority of the industry." / "\"we found empirical evidence for the existence of demographic differentials in the majority of the face recognition algorithms we studied,\"" / "For one-to-many matching, the team saw higher rates of false positives for African American females." / "(In this case, the test did not use the entire set of photos, but only one FBI database containing 1.6 million domestic mugshots.)" / "those that are the most equitable also rank among the most accurate." / "The team did not use any images \"scraped\" directly from internet sources such as social media or from video surveillance."

## Claims

C1. HUD's coordinated entry notice CPD-17-01, issued Jan. 23, 2017, defines prioritization as ranking everyone who uses coordinated entry "in order of priority" and requires that people with the most severe needs be prioritized first "to the maximum extent feasible."
- Source: Notice CPD-17-01, Notice Establishing Additional Requirements for a Continuum of Care Centralized or Coordinated Assessment System, HUD Office of Community Planning and Development, Jan. 23, 2017 — https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/17-01cpdn.pdf
- Quote: "Issued: January 23, 2017 / Expires: This Notice is effective until it is amended, superseded, or rescinded" ... "HUD uses the term 'Prioritization' to refer to the coordinated entry-specific process by which all persons in need of assistance who use coordinated entry are ranked in order of priority." ... "the coordinated entry process must, to the maximum extent feasible, ensure that people with more severe service needs and levels of vulnerability are prioritized for housing and homeless assistance before those with less severe service needs and lower levels of vulnerability."
- Last checked for newer developments: Searched for a 2025 or 2026 amendment or rescission; none found. HUD's FY2026 renewal instructions (C4) still cite it.

C2. CPD-17-01 requires that prioritization policies be documented and made publicly available.
- Source: same as C1 — https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/17-01cpdn.pdf

C8. A 2019 C4 Innovations analysis of coordinated entry data from four Continuums of Care (Portland/Multnomah County, Ore.; Roanoke, Va.; Seattle/King County and Tacoma/Pierce County, Wash.) found that clients of color received statistically significantly lower VI-SPDAT scores than white clients, and that the tool's subscales tilted toward vulnerabilities white clients were more likely to report.
- Source: Coordinated Entry Systems Racial Equity Analysis of Assessment Data, C4 Innovations (Wilkey, Donegan, Yampolskaya, Cannon), prepared for Building Changes, October 2019 — https://www.niwrc.org/sites/default/files/files/reports/CES_Racial_Equity_Analysis_2019-.pdf
- Quote: "Coordinated Entry assessment data were collected from four partner communities: Portland-Gresham-Multnomah County CoC in Oregon; Roanoke City and County/Salem CoC in Virginia; Seattle/King County CoC in Washington; and Tacoma/Lakewood/Pierce County CoC in Washington." ... "On average, BIPOC clients receive statistically significantly lower prioritization scores on the VI-SPDAT than their White counterparts" ... "VI-SPDAT subscales do not equitably capture vulnerabilities for BIPOC compared to Whites: race is a predictor of 11/16 subscales, and most subscales are tilted towards capturing vulnerabilities that Whites are more likely to endorse." ... "While the difference is small, the results are statistically significant"
- Denominator: single adults N = 18,793; families N = 6,065. For families, "race was not a statistically significant predictor of receiving a higher prioritization score in the aggregate dataset."
- Caution: the report's "32% less likely" figure is an odds-ratio interpretation (note: "1/.758 = 1.32"); not used in essay.
- Last checked: no newer C4 study found; later work (C10, C13) points the same direction.

C9. Marybeth Shinn and Molly Richard, writing in the American Journal of Public Health in 2022, called the VI-SPDAT the most widely used tool until recently, noted its creators stopped supporting it, and urged systems to be explicit about the value choices in allocating scarce resources rather than outsourcing them to an instrument.
- Source: Shinn M, Richard MK, "Allocating Homeless Services After the Withdrawal of the Vulnerability Index–Service Prioritization Decision Assistance Tool," Am J Public Health 2022;112(3):378–382 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8887175/
- Affiliation (from article page): "Marybeth Shinn and Molly K. Richard are with Peabody College, Vanderbilt University, Nashville, TN."
- Quote: "Until recently, the most widely used tool was the VI-SPDAT (Vulnerability Index–Service Prioritization Decision Assistance Tool) for single adults or a modification for youths and families." ... "Ultimately, widespread concerns led the VI-SPDAT's creators to stop supporting its use." ... "systems should be explicit about the value choices inherent in the allocation of scarce resources, rather than outsourcing decisions to an instrument." ... "no single principle can allocate scarce interventions justly."
- Last checked: Oct. 19, 2026.

C10. A Los Angeles research project (CESTTRR, led at USC with UCLA's California Policy Lab data team and the Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority) measured the VI-SPDAT against a community-defined outcome (jail, hospitalization, psychiatric holds, death) and found an AUC of 0.54, against 0.50 for random guessing; a model optimized only for accuracy reached 0.64; the final equity-adjusted 19-question tool reached 0.60 and cut racial gaps in its error measure from as high as 8.5 percent to under 1 percent. A model using about 100 features from integrated county data reached 0.831.
- Source: Coordinated Entry System Triage Tool Research and Refinement (CESTTRR) Final Report, USC Center for AI in Society et al., 2023 — https://cais.usc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/CESTTRR-Final-Report-2023.pdf
- Quote: "we found that the VI-SPDAT has an AUC of 0.54 while random guessing is reflected as an AUC of 0.5. After optimizing a model for accuracy only, we were able to identify a triage tool with an AUC of 0.64." ... "the final revised prediction tool we identified and presented to the CPG and CAB has an AUC of 0.60." ... "Overall, we were able to identify a revised triage tool that increased both accuracy (from an AUC of 0.54 to an AUC of 0.60) and equity (from GFNR gaps as high as 8.5% to GFNR gaps less than 1% across included groups)." ... "The Revised Triage Tool is only 19 questions based on a data science model that predicts "adverse events" using HMIS and county ELP data. The VI-SPDAT is a long questionnaire containing 35 multiple choice questions" ... "identify "adverse outcomes" (e.g., jail, hospitalizations, psychiatric holds, death) as definition of a measurable vulnerability outcome captured in county data records." ... "The AUC for this hypothetical model is 0.831"
- Partners: "Eric Rice, PhD served as the Project Lead: Professor at the USC Suzanne Dworak-Peck School of Social Work"; data team includes "Research Director of the California Policy Lab at UCLA."
- Caution: "bias" here is a specific metric (gaps in Generalized False Negative Rate by race), and the summary elsewhere gives 5.9 percent to 0.7 percent for Black clients. The essay uses only the "as high as 8.5% to less than 1%" framing, attributed.
- Last checked: LAHSA page (C12) confirms the tool is in use as of Aug. 17, 2026.

C12. Los Angeles adopted the resulting Los Angeles Housing Assessment Tool (LA HAT) alongside the VI-SPDAT; from December 2025 to April 2026, applicants were more likely to get an eligible score on the VI-SPDAT, providers chose the VI-SPDAT, and LAHSA says this "perpetuated the racial bias of the VI-SPDAT"; the CES Policy Council lowered the LA HAT threshold from 17 to 12 on April 22, 2026, and planned to consider retiring the VI-SPDAT for supportive housing in fall 2026.
- Source: "Los Angeles Housing Assessment Tool (LA HAT)," Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority, published April 22, 2025, last updated Aug. 17, 2026 — https://www.lahsa.org/news?article=1033-los-angeles-housing-assessment-tool-la-hat-
- Quote: "The Los Angeles Coordinated Entry System (CES) for Adults is transitioning to the Los Angeles Housing Assessment Tool ( LA HAT ), which will eventually fully replace the Vulnerability Index - Service Prioritization Decision Assistance Tool" ... "developed by the CES Triage Tool Research and Refinement (CESTTRR) Project" ... "Between December 2025 and April 2026, CES Acuity Scores of 8+ on the VI-SPDAT and 17+ on the LA HAT served as eligible scores for PSH consideration. However, initial quantitative data and feedback from direct services providers reflected that these threshold scores yielded a disparate rate of eligible participants for PSH Consideration, with participants being more likely to obtain an eligible score when assessed with the VI-SPDAT than with the LA HAT. This trend perpetuated the racial bias of the VI-SPDAT in the System and slowed down the implementation of the LA HAT as direct services providers opted for administering the VI-SPDAT over the LA HAT." ... "Threshold Scores Lowered from 17 + to 12+ on the LA HAT" ... "on April 22, 2026" ... "The next expected system improvement related to the LA HAT will be in the Fall of 2026 when the CES Policy Council will consider the process and timeline by which the VI-SPDAT will no longer be valid for PSH consideration."
- Last checked: page updated Aug. 17, 2026; no fall 2026 decision posted as of Oct. 19, 2026.

C13. A study of Allegheny County, Pa.'s algorithmic replacement for the VI-SPDAT found the VI-SPDAT favored white clients and that, after the new tool, scores were similar across racial groups but disparities in service rates did not clearly decrease.
- Source: Cheng, Drayton, Chouldechova, Vaithianathan, "Algorithm-Assisted Decision Making and Racial Disparities in Housing: A Study of the Allegheny Housing Assessment Tool," arXiv:2407.21209, submitted July 30, 2024, revised Oct. 22, 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21209
- Quote: "We find that the VI-SPDAT tended to assign higher risk scores to white clients and lower risk scores to Black clients, and that white clients were served at a higher rates pre-AHA deployment. While post-deployment service decisions became better aligned with the AHA score, and the distribution of AHA scores is similar across racial groups, we do not find evidence of a corresponding decrease in disparities in service rates. We attribute the persistent disparity to the use of Alt-AHA, a survey-based tool that is used in cases of low data quality, as well as group differences in eligibility-related factors, such as chronic homelessness and veteran status."
- Caution: preprint (arXiv), not confirmed peer-reviewed. Attributed as a study by the named researchers.
- Last checked: Oct. 19, 2026 (v3 is current on arXiv).

C14. New York City's coordinated entry manual (NYC CoC, NY-600) says the Standardized Vulnerability Assessment (SVA) is run on all approved supportive housing applications and sorts applicants into high, medium and low vulnerability based on Medicaid service use in the past year or multi-system contacts plus functional impairments in the past two years; chronically homeless applicants are then ranked by SVA level, then by cumulative days homeless in the past three years, with application date as tiebreaker.
- Source: NYC Coalition on the Continuum of Care NY-600, Coordinated Entry System Policies and Procedures for the NYC Coordinated Assessment and Placement System (CAPS), August 2019 — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nycccoc/downloads/pdf/CAPS_Policies%20_Procedures%20_8.19_final.pdf
- Quote: "The Standardized Vulnerability Assessment (SVA) is conducted on all approved supportive housing applications submitted in PACTWeb." ... "The SVA uses a categorical system of High, Medium, and Low Vulnerability. The SVA service need categories are defined as participants that meet the threshold for Medicaid service utilization within the past year OR participants with multi-system contacts and functional impairments within the last 2 years, always designating the higher category." ... "Priority #1: People who meet the HUD definition of chronic homelessness and have been determined to be Level I (high vulnerability) based on New York City's SVA, which consider length of time homeless, multi-system contact, and functional impairment/high utilization of Medicaid." ... "Within each priority group described above, participants will be prioritized based on the number of days of cumulative homelessness during the past 3 years." ... "Only as necessary to break a tie, participants within each priority group will be further prioritized based on date of application"
- Last checked: This is the most recent manual posted that I found. The July 2026 FAQ (C16) confirms the SVA is still the prioritization system. I could not confirm whether thresholds have changed since 2019.

C25. White v. Massachusetts Council of Construction Employers (1983): Boston's order requiring city-funded projects to use at least half city residents did not violate the Commerce Clause where the city spent its own funds, as a market participant.
- Source: White v. Massachusetts Council of Construction Employers, 460 U.S. 204 (1983), Cornell LII — https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/460/204
- Quote: "Petitioner Mayor of Boston, Mass., issued an executive order requiring all construction projects funded in whole or in part by city funds or funds that the city had authority to administer to be performed by a work force at least half of which are bona fide residents of the city." / "Insofar as the city expended only its own funds in entering into construction contracts for public projects, it was a market participant and entitled to be treated as such under the rule of Hughes v. Alexandria Scrap Corp."
- Note: the market-participant doctrine limits dormant Commerce Clause challenges; it does not bar Congress from preempting. Essay says so.

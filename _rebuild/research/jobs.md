## Claims

C1. Since March 2025, New York's WARN filing system has asked employers whether "technological innovation or automation" contributed to a layoff, via a checkbox; if checked, the employer is asked to name the technology.
- Source: Robert T. Quackenboss, "New York WARN Act: No AI-Related Layoffs Reported in First Year of Adding AI-Related Disclosure to the System," Hunton Andrews Kurth, via National Law Review, May 18, 2026 — https://natlawreview.com/article/new-york-warn-act-no-ai-related-layoffs-reported-first-year-adding-ai-related
- Quote: "Specifically, when filing WARN notices, employers are now asked whether "technological innovation or automation" contributed to the reported layoffs. Employers report this by simply marking a checkbox on the form. If the box is checked, the employer is asked to name the technology responsible. The NYS DOL added the new AI-related disclosure to the system in March 2025."
- Last checked for newer developments: searched for any 2026 reporting of employers checking the box; found none beyond C3 (search budget ran out after this check; see "Could not confirm").

C2. In the year after the change, more than 160 companies filed WARN notices in New York, including Amazon and Goldman Sachs, and none attributed layoffs to technological innovation or automation, according to a state Labor Department spokesperson.
- Source: Khorri Atkinson, "AI-Related Layoffs Test New York's Ability to Track Job Losses," Bloomberg Tax, March 10, 2026 — https://news.bloombergtax.com/payroll/ai-related-layoffs-test-new-yorks-ability-to-track-job-losses
- Quote: "More than 160 companies filed mass termination notices with New York's Department of Labor in the year following a first-of-its-kind update to the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) law requiring disclosure of AI impact on the cuts. But none of the notices—including from Amazon.com Inc. and Goldman Sachs Group Inc. which have integrated AI technology in their business operations—attributed layoffs to "technological innovation or automation," an agency spokesperson confirmed."
- Also: Hunton (C1 source): "Even though more than 160 different companies have filed WARN notices with the NYS DOL, not a single notice has attributed layoffs to AI technology or automation."
- Last checked: see C3, which updates this through late May 2026.

C3. The state Labor Department told Princeton researchers Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor that as of late May 2026 one company, Nespresso, had checked the box; by their calculation that covered 46 of about 25,000 laid-off New York workers in the period.
- Source: Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor, "Why AI hasn't replaced software engineers, and won't," Normal Tech (newsletter), June 11, 2026 — https://www.normaltech.ai/p/why-ai-hasnt-replaced-software-engineers
- Quote: "In the full first year, more than 160 companies filed WARN notices. Not a single one checked the AI box. We reached out to the NY Department of Labor who confirmed that as of late May, only one company, Nespresso, checked the box. If these filings are accurate, only 46 out of about 25,000 laid off workers in New York State in the relevant period, or about two-tenths of a percent, were affected by AI."
- Footnote quote: "it is possible that companies are under-reporting AI as a reason for layoffs because of ambiguity or asymmetric risks from checking versus not checking the box (though we have no specific reason to think this)."
- Note: the "Princeton" affiliation is not stated on this page; essay identifies them only as researchers who write the Normal Tech newsletter. (Their acknowledgments credit "our colleague Mihir Kshirsagar.")
- Last checked: no newer count found before search cap.

C4. The WARN statute itself was not amended for the AI question, and employers lack guidance on whether it is mandatory and how "technological innovation or automation" is defined.
- Source: Hunton / National Law Review, May 18, 2026 (C1 URL)
- Quote: "As the statutory language of New York's WARN Act was not amended to reflect the request for AI-related disclosure, employers lack guidance and may question if the disclosure is optional or mandatory. Employers also may lack clarity on how "technological innovation or automation" is defined and conclude their AI-use does not fall within the category."

C5. Kevin Frazier of the Abundance Institute told Bloomberg Tax the WARN Act is "a product of the 1970s."
- Source: Bloomberg Tax, March 10, 2026 (C2 URL)
- Quote: "New York's approach is "well intentioned," but "fundamentally, the WARN Act is a product of the 1970s," when factory closings were geographically confined and occurred over days or weeks, said Kevin Frazier, a senior fellow at the Abundance Institute."

- Quote: "the shortfall was 15% at the July 2025 data vintage and is 19% as of June 2026. The adjustment appears to operate primarily through reduced hiring of young workers rather than increased separations."

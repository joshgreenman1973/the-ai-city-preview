# Leads (unverified until you fetch them)

These come from a Vital City data-story draft checked against sources on Sept. 23, 2026. They are good starting points. They are NOT yet verified for your essay: fetch each source, copy the quote, and check for anything newer before you use it.

## Inside City Hall (the-inventory, the-desk, front-door, schools, acs-model)
- Local Law 35 of 2022 requires agencies to report algorithmic tools; inventory on NYC Open Data, dataset jaw4-yuem (https://data.cityofnewyork.us/City-Government/Algorithmic-Tools-Compliance-Report/jaw4-yuem). Counts: 31 tools (2022), 46 (2023), 55 (2024), 86 (2025); 19 agencies in 2025; Health Department most (17 in 2025), Mayor's Office 12. 2025 PDF: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/oti/downloads/pdf/reports/LL35%20Report%202025%20-%20Final%20-%202026-03-27.pdf (the PDF miscites the law as "Local Law 35 of 2021"; it is 2022).
- 2025 autonomy ratings: 40 informative, 23 supervised, 11 monitored, 12 fully autonomous; 7 of the 12 are captioning/transcription/Google Translate; others include FloodVision, a youth-department chatbot, MyCity.
- ACS model trained on about 815,000 records from investigations closed 2012–2022; picks which of about 50,000 investigations a year get a second review (from jaw4-yuem).
- Schools: algebra teaching assistant (generative AI) live May 2023; schools blocked ChatGPT Jan. 2023 (https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2023/1/3/23537987/nyc-schools-ban-chatgpt-writing-artificial-intelligence/); Banks reversed May 2023 (https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2023/5/18/23727942/chatgpt-nyc-schools-david-banks/). Sept. 2, 2026: Mamdani administration paused student-facing generative AI for a year, 2-K through 8th grade, nearly 600,000 students (https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/09/mayor-mamdani-and-chancellor-samuels-put-students-first-with-nat). Pew Feb. 24, 2026: 1 in 10 teens do all or most schoolwork with chatbots' help (https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/).
- AI Action Plan, Oct. 2023, 37 actions (https://www.nyc.gov/assets/oti/downloads/pdf/reports/artificial-intelligence-action-plan.pdf); Oct. 2025 progress report: 27 complete, 8 in progress, 2 not started (https://www.nyc.gov/assets/oti/downloads/pdf/AI-Action-Plan-Progress-Report-Year-2.pdf).
- MyCity chatbot gave illegal advice (The Markup, March 29, 2024: https://themarkup.org/news/2024/03/29/nycs-ai-chatbot-tells-businesses-to-break-the-law). Mamdani took it down in Feb. 2026, citing about $500,000 cost (https://themarkup.org/artificial-intelligence/2026/01/30/mamdani-to-kill-the-nyc-ai-chatbot-we-caught-telling-businesses-to-break-the-law).
- City & State, July 2026: spokesperson Jeremy Edwards, AI "only where it demonstrably improves life for New Yorkers"; Office of Algorithmic Accountability (created by a 2025 law) "was supposed to be created by June" (https://www.cityandstateny.com/policy/2026/07/mamdanis-government-has-started-embrace-ai-will-he/414872/). Check whether it exists now.
- Federal inventory 3,611 AI use cases (2025) vs 710 (2023) (https://github.com/ombegov/2025-Federal-Agency-AI-Use-Case-Inventory). NY State first inventory Dec. 30, 2025: 19 systems at 13 agencies (https://data.ny.gov/Government-Finance/NYS-AI-Systems-Inventory-Beginning-September-2025/fgvt-qr4i); New York Focus: omits DMV facial recognition (https://nysfocus.com/2026/07/30/new-york-ai-systems-inventory-findings).
- Code for America April 2026 state assessment: 11 states "advanced" on readiness, none on impact (https://files.codeforamerica.org/2026/04/30110659/government-ai-landscape-assessment-2026-research-report-final.pdf).
- City language access report FY25: 1.5 million+ phone interpretations; "some agencies noted reliance on unsupervised machine translation tools..." (https://www.nyc.gov/assets/immigrants/downloads/pdf/FY25-Language-Access-Annual-Report.pdf).
- A law effective January 2026 requires the City's technology office to study how algorithmic tools affect the civil service (https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=6874682&GUID=0B6A4E8B-4D49-437B-BB70-50B898D2BD7F).
- 2025: three AI laws and a facial recognition law became law without Mayor Adams' signature, including the Office of Algorithmic Accountability (https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=6557537&GUID=14A351D1-8AED-448E-A8C0-355A39CB02BE) and a public list of assessed AI systems (https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=6860202&GUID=C8F189B6-FFA8-4539-86BF-78436E7BE335). Council hearing on AI risks Oct. 5, 2026 (check what happened). Local laws: 49 of 2018, 35 of 2022, 144 of 2021, 55, 188, 193, 195 of 2025, 25 of 2026.

## Work and money (jobs, fiscal)
- Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey: 16.8% of New York metro firms reported using AI (six rounds through early Sept. 2026) vs 21.9% nationally; lowest of 25 largest metros; San Diego 30.2% (https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/data_downloads). Current question began Nov. 17, 2025; do not join to the older series.
- City Comptroller Mark Levine, "AI and New York City's Fiscal Future," May 21, 2026 (https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/AI-and-NYCs-Fiscal-Future.pdf): college grads 22–27 unemployment 7.3% vs 7.1% for non-grads, year ending March 2026, first time on record; baseline ~52,000 private jobs a year; "AI Shockwave" scenario (5% probability) private employment 259,000 below baseline by early 2029; employment-weighted national adoption ~32%.
- NY WARN automation checkbox since March 2025; 160+ companies filed by May 2026, per Hunton Andrews Kurth (https://natlawreview.com/article/new-york-warn-act-no-ai-related-layoffs-reported-first-year-adding-ai-related); State DOL told Normal Tech only Nespresso had checked the box as of late May (https://www.normaltech.ai/p/why-ai-hasnt-replaced-software-engineers). Bloomberg Tax: https://news.bloombergtax.com/payroll/ai-related-layoffs-test-new-yorks-ability-to-track-job-losses
- Anthropic exposure measure: programmers 0.75, customer service reps 0.70, general office clerks 0.45 (https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts). NY metro: ~125,600 customer service reps, ~136,600 general office clerks, May 2025 (https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes_35620.htm). Note: Anthropic makes the model writing this issue — disclose if used, or prefer an independent measure.
- QCEW five boroughs, March 2026: computer systems design 80,732; software publishers 33,435; securities 201,470.
- State Comptroller: NY metro startups raised $28.5B across 2,195 deals in 2024 (https://www.osc.ny.gov/files/reports/osdc/pdf/report-13-2026.pdf).

## Grid (data-centers)
- NYISO large-load queue: 6 projects / 1,045 MW (2022) → 51 projects / ~12,670 MW (May 1, 2026) (https://www.nyiso.com/documents/d/guest/2026-power-trends). 2026 Gold Book: large-load demand 538 MW (2026) → 2,880 MW summer peak by 2040; zero in Zone J (NYC) every year (https://www.nyiso.com/documents/20142/2226333/2026-Gold-Book-Public.pdf/a8fd42fe-5a8c-88cb-5052-f93dfd6423b8?t=1777471793334).
- Con Edison Jan. 2026 reliability report: shortfall 125 MW in 2032 rising to 750 MW by 2036; does not mention data centers (https://www.coned.com/-/media/files/coned/documents/business-partners/business-opportunities/non-emitting-reliability-solutions/reliability-needs-report.pdf).
- NY residential price 29.5 cents/kWh June 2026 vs 18.3 nationally (EIA-861M).
- July 2026: Hochul paused State environmental permits for new hyperscale data centers for up to a year (https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/first-statewide-moratorium-new-hyperscale-data-centers-launched-governor-kathy-hochul). Siena Sept. 23, 2026: NYC voters oppose large data centers nearby 62–23 (https://sri.siena.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/SNY-September-2026-Poll-Crosstabs-9-23-26.pdf).

## Rules and watchdogs (landlord-algorithm, face-and-plate, risk-scores, preemption)
- Local Law 144 (hiring tools), enforced since July 5, 2023 (https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/automated-employment-decision-tools.page). Cornell/Data & Society 2024 study: 391 employers, 18 audit reports, 13 notices (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01399). State Comptroller audit Dec. 2, 2025: City received 2 complaints in 2 years; City surveyed 32 companies, found 1 issue; State auditors found at least 17 instances of potential noncompliance (https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools).
- POST Act (2020) (https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=3343878&GUID=996ABB2A-9F4C-4A32-B081-D6F24AB954A0); NYPD has published 41 policies (https://www.nyc.gov/site/nypd/about/about-nypd/policy/post-act.page). DOI inspector general May 2024: NYPD had rejected 14 of 15 recommendations from its 2022 review (https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doi/reports/pdf/2024/25PostActRelease_Rpt_05_30_2024.pdf). NYPD facial recognition figures date from 2019: 9,850 requests, 2,510 possible matches (https://www.nyc.gov/site/nypd/about/about-nypd/equipment-tech/facial-recognition.page). 2025 law requires annual NYPD facial recognition audit (https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=6557579&GUID=CBBA0BE3-696A-4A94-A212-46163F1FED29) — check whether the first one is out.
- Comptroller ShotSpotter audit: most alerts in audited months not confirmed as gunfire (https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/audit-report-on-the-new-york-city-police-departments-oversight-of-its-agreement-with-shotspotter-inc-for-the-gunshot-detection-and-location-system).
- RAISE Act signed Dec. 2025, amended March 2026; largest developers report safety incidents within 72 hours; oversight office in Department of Financial Services; effective Jan. 1, 2027 (https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-signs-nation-leading-legislation-require-ai-frameworks-ai-frontier-models; https://www.wiley.law/alert-New-York-Finalizes-RAISE-Act-for-Frontier-AI-Models-Law-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027).
- Pew: share "more concerned than excited" 37% (2021), 52% (2023), 50% (June 2025) (https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/03/12/key-findings-about-how-americans-view-artificial-intelligence/).
